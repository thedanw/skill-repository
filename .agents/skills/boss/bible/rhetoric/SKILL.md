# Rhetoric Skill

## Goal

To provide a comprehensive set of rhetorical and literary devices to enhance clarity, memorability, and impact in sermon communication.

## Homiletical Creative Tactics

These are the primary devices used for sermon outline brainstorming.

- **(P) Picture**: Use a word picture, metaphor, or overarching image to anchor the text.
- **(R) Rhyme**: Use assonance, poetry, or rhyming phrases to make points "sticky."
- **(E) Echo**: Repeat a specific phrase or "refrain" across multiple points.
- **(A) Alliteration**: Use rhythmic consonant sounds at the start of multiple words/points.
- **(C) Contrast**: juxtapose opposing ideas (e.g., "The Law's Demand vs. The Gospel's Delivery").
- **(H) Hook**: Use a lyrical "riff," catchy phrase, or curiosity-driven titles.
- **(D) Direct**: Logical, propositional statements with no specific literary device.

## Classical Rhetorical Devices

Use these to add depth to illustrations, application, and specific teaching points.

1.  **Alliteration**: Repetition of initial consonant sounds (e.g., "wild and woolly").
2.  **Anacoluthon**: Syntactical inconsistency or shift in a sentence (e.g., "you really should have—well, what do you expect?").
3.  **Anadiplosis**: Repetition of the last word of one phrase at the beginning of the next (e.g., "rely on his honor—honor such as his?").
4.  **Analepsis**: A flashback or interruption of chronological sequence.
5.  **Anaphora**: Repetition of a word at the beginning of successive phrases (e.g., "we cannot dedicate—we cannot consecrate").
6.  **Antanaclasis**: Repetition of a word with a different meaning (e.g., "hang together or hang separately").
7.  **Antiphrasis**: Ironic or humorous use of words in opposite senses (e.g., "this giant of 3 feet 4 inches").
8.  **Antonomasia**: Using a proper name for a class (a "Solomon" for wise) or a title for a name (the "Bard").
9.  **Apophasis**: Raising an issue by claiming not to mention it (e.g., "we won't discuss his past crimes").
10. **Aporia**: Expression of doubt or uncertainty for effect (e.g., "to be, or not to be").
11. **Cacophony**: Harshness in the sound of words.
12. **Chiasmus**: Inverted relationship between parallel phrases (e.g., "working hard or hardly working").
13. **Dialogism**: Disjunctive conclusion from a single premise.
14. **Dysphemism**: Substituting a disagreeable expression for an inoffensive one (e.g., "greasy spoon" for "diner").
15. **Epistrophe**: Repetition of a word/phrase at the end of successive clauses (e.g., "of the people, by the people, for the people").
16. **Epizeuxis**: Emphatic repetition of a single word.
17. **Hypallage**: Interchange of two elements from logical to less logical relationship (e.g., "you are lost to joy for joy is lost to you").
18. **Hyperbaton**: Inversion of idiomatic word order (e.g., "judge me by my size, do you?").
19. **Hyperbole**: Extravagant exaggeration (e.g., "mile-high ice-cream cones").
20. **Hypophora**: Putting or answering an objection against one's own contention.
21. **Litotes**: Understatement expressed by the negative of the contrary (e.g., "not a bad singer").
22. **Meiosis**: Presentation of a thing with underemphasis (understatement).
23. **Metaphor**: A figure of speech suggesting a likeness between two things (e.g., "drowning in money").
24. **Metonymy**: Use of the name of one thing for another it is associated with (e.g., "the crown" for the monarchy).
25. **Onomatopoeia**: Naming a thing by imitation of its sound (e.g., "buzz").
26. **Oxymoron**: Combination of contradictory words (e.g., "cruel kindness").
27. **Pleonasm**: Use of redundant words (e.g., "I saw it with my own eyes").
28. **Simile**: Comparison using "like" or "as" (e.g., "cheeks like roses").
29. **Syllepsis**: A word relating to two others in different senses (literal and metaphorical).
30. **Synecdoche**: A part representing the whole (e.g., "fifty sail" for fifty ships).
31. **Zeugma**: A word governing two or more others in different senses (e.g., "opened the door and her heart").
